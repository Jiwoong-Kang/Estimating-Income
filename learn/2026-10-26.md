- Chose k with Elbow (full data) + Silhouette (5,000 sample), then fit k=2 and looked at `>50K` rates per cluster.

### 2. What clustering can / cannot tell you

- **Can:** groups of similar people; whether high-income rates differ by group → whether income-related **structure** exists
- **Cannot:** classification accuracy ranking, or “this person must be >50K” as direct supervised prediction (labels were not used in fit)

### 3. Elbow vs Silhouette

| Method | Data | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Elbow (inertia) | Full | Spot a knee for a sense of k |
| Silhouette | 5,000 sample | −1–1; higher = cleaner clusters |

- Why not silhouette on full data: roughly ~n² cost
- Fit Elbow and silhouette `KMeans` **separately** (need labels on the sample)

### 4. Choosing k

- Silhouette: **k=2 best** (~0.38). k≥3 drops sharply
- Elbow: inertia keeps falling; knee is **fuzzy**
- Decision: report “follow silhouette → **k=2**; Elbow was unclear”

### 5. High-income rate by cluster (k=2)

| cluster | n | >50K rate (%) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 29,855 | 20.6 |
| 1 | 2,706 | **62.0** |
| Overall | 32,561 | 24.1 |

- Target y was **not** passed to `fit`; rates computed only after clustering
- Small cluster (1) has 62% high income — much above overall
- → Unsupervised grouping still shows income-related structure
- But cluster 1 ≠ “all high income” (38% still ≤50K). Do not equate to classification scores.
