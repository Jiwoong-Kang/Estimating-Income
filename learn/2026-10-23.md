### 2. Why not `degree=3` — stick with `2` (report rationale)

After preprocessing, ~**71** columns:

| degree | Approx. columns | Train matrix (rough, float64) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | ~2,600 | ~0.7 GB |
| 3 | ~65,000 | ~17 GB |

- One deg=2 fit is a few seconds.
- deg=3 expands columns by tens of times → **memory** first, and even with fewer rows training takes minutes → full 30k × 5-fold CV is **tens of minutes–hours or OOM**.
- Conclusion: on this data/pipeline **`degree=3` is impractical → choose `degree=2`**. Report: “tried tuning but narrowed candidates for cost.”

### 4. Lessons from Perceptron tuning

- Knob: with `penalty="l2"`, **`alpha` = L2 strength**
  - Small `alpha` → weak penalty / large `alpha` → smaller weights (too large → underfitting)
- With `penalty=None`, `alpha` is unused — turn on `l2` when tuning.
- Candidates: `[1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-1]`, select by CV **F1 mean**
- If the final cell builds a **new** model, `clone` is unnecessary (clone only when the same object is shared across two pipes)

### 5. Perceptron CV (`alpha` candidates)

| alpha | CV F1 mean (±std) |
|---|---|
| **1e-4** | **0.540 (±0.162)** ← chosen |
| 1e-3 | 0.513 (±0.127) |
| 1e-2 | 0.378 (±0.136) |

- Larger `alpha` hurt F1 → weak regularization best in this range.
- Chosen: **`alpha=0.0001`**

- Fixed: `dual=False`, `max_iter=5000`, `random_state=42`
- Candidates: `[0.01, 0.1, 1, 10]`, select by CV **F1 mean**

### 10. Lessons from k-NN tuning

- Knob: **`n_neighbors` (k)** — how many neighbors vote
  - Small k → noise-sensitive / large k → may lean majority class
- Candidates are **odd** (`5, 11, 15, 25, 35`) to reduce ties in binary classification. Even k is allowed but odd is common.
- Distance-based → keep **scaling** (pipeline default).
- ~30k samples → slow CV (candidates × 5-fold).
- Code/save OK. Final k saved as `knn_k{k}.json`.
